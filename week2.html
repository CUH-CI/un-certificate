Week 2 - The Pacific and CI Tools

Sustainability in the Pacific

Module 2A

🪝 Hook

Small Island Developing States make up less than 1% of global greenhouse gas emissions - yet they face some of the most severe consequences of climate change. Rising seas, intensifying storms, shrinking freshwater supplies, and collapsing fisheries aren’t abstract future risks for Pacific communities. They are present-day realities.

But Pacific island nations are not passive victims. They are active leaders in sustainability action - combining traditional ecological knowledge, community-based approaches, and scientific tools to adapt and respond. The question this module explores is: what does sustainability actually mean in this context, and how does data and cyberinfrastructure affect those efforts?

📖 Recommended reading: About Small Island Developing States (SIDS) - Read to learn about what the Small Island Developing States are, who is it home to, challenges they face, and more.

🌏 Hands-On Use Case - UNDP SIDS Data Platform

The UNDP SIDS Data Platform is a cyberinfrastructure tool built specifically for Small Island Developing States - including country-level sustainability indicators, a GIS portal with geospatial datasets, and a Multidimensional Vulnerability Index across all SIDS regions including the Pacific.

Your task this module:

Visit the UNDP SIDS Data Platform and explore the data available for your chosen Pacific island country.

What to look for:

  • Find your island country’s profile - what sustainability indicators are tracked?
  • Look at the Multidimensional Vulnerability Index (MVI) - where does your island rank and why?
  • What data is missing or flagged as unavailable for your island?
  • How does this platform compare to the Pacific Data Hub you explored in Week 1?

🌊 Hands-On Use Case - PacIOOS

PacIOOS (Pacific Islands Ocean Observing System) is a real-time ocean and coastal monitoring network - buoys, wave sensors, and tide gauges - operating across Hawaiʻi and the Pacific Islands. It’s a working example of a CI tool stack in action: sensors collect data, transmit it via satellite, store it in the cloud, and visualize it in a live dashboard accessible from any browser.

Your task this module:

Visit the PacIOOS data viewer and explore sensor data near your chosen island country.

What to look for:

  • Find a sensor feed relevant to a sustainability challenge your island faces (may not include data for your specific island, but beneficial to explore what’s out there!)
  • What does the data show - what is being measured and how often?
  • Can you find any gaps in coverage near your island?
  • How does the data get from the sensor to your screen? Think about the CI stack concept from the lecture.

📋 Mini Assignment - CI Tool Stack

Week 2 Assignment - CI Tool Stack

Using what you explored in the PacIOOS hands-on use case and the lecture, design a simple CI tool stack for the sustainability challenge from Week 1 for your chosen island country.

Complete the five-field template:

  1. Sustainability challenge: What problem are you monitoring? (e.g. coastal erosion in Tuvalu)
  2. What to measure: Which variables matter? (e.g. wave height, sea surface temperature, population)
  3. Tool or sensor type: What would collect or process this data? (e.g. coastal buoy, satellite imagery, open data portal)
  4. Data transmission: How does data get from the source to a usable format? (e.g. satellite uplink, cloud storage, open API)
  5. Constraint or trade-off: One real infrastructure barrier affecting your design (e.g. limited bandwidth, no reliable power grid, no institutional email for tool access)

Add your responses to the Week 2 section of your Pacific CI Portfolio Google Doc. When you’re done, submit your portfolio link in Google Classroom.
